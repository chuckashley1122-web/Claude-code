Ideal SureTest 61-165: what it does and where it falls short

Answers questions about a receptacle circuit in seconds that would otherwise take an hour of investigation.

Category
Equipment — Branch circuit analyser
Starting price
Street price approx. $300–$450
Best for
Troubleshooting receptacle circuits and documenting circuit condition quickly
Not a good fit for
Hardwired circuits and panels without receptacles

What it does

The SureTest plugs into a receptacle and performs a set of measurements a multimeter cannot easily give you. It applies a test load and measures the resulting voltage drop, which reveals undersized conductors, long runs, loose connections and shared neutrals that read perfectly fine at no load. It also measures line and ground impedance, detects wiring faults including reversed polarity and a false ground, and tests GFCI trip time and trip current.

The value is diagnostic speed. Intermittent problems on branch circuits — equipment misbehaving, lights dimming, a circuit that works until it is loaded — are exactly the faults an unloaded voltage reading hides. A plug-in test that shows excessive voltage drop under load turns a vague complaint into a located problem, which is why it earns its place on a service van.

Who it suits

This suits electricians doing receptacle-level troubleshooting and verification.

  • Service electricians chasing intermittent faults and equipment problems on branch circuits.
  • Commissioning and verification work needing documented voltage drop and impedance values.
  • GFCI and wiring-fault verification across many receptacles quickly.
  • Contractors investigating complaints in older buildings with unknown wiring history.

Where it falls short

It is a specialised tool with a narrow access point.

  • It works through receptacles. Hardwired equipment, lighting circuits and panel-level testing are outside what it can reach without an adapter or a different approach.
  • Results need interpreting. A voltage drop figure alone does not tell you whether the cause is conductor size, run length or a loose connection — that is still your diagnosis to make.
  • Single-purpose instrument at a real price, which is harder to justify for a shop doing mostly new installation.
  • Its test load means you should think about what is on the circuit before testing, particularly with sensitive equipment connected.
  • GFCI trip testing repeatedly on old devices can finish off a device that was already failing, which you then own.

Before you buy

Decide whether your work is troubleshooting or installation. This tool pays back on diagnostics and sits unused on new construction.

Learn what the impedance and voltage drop figures mean before using the results in front of a customer.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
