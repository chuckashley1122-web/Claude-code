What it does

The SureTest plugs into a receptacle and tests what a simple three-light tester cannot: voltage drop under simulated load, true impedance, GFCI trip time and trip current, false ground and bootleg neutral conditions, and correct AFCI operation. It finds the loose neutral and the undersized run that read fine unloaded.

Who it suits

Service electricians troubleshooting intermittent problems, anyone doing inspection or due-diligence work on existing wiring, and techs verifying GFCI and AFCI devices to a documented standard.

Where it falls short

It only works on 120V receptacles, so it cannot help with 240V circuits, hardwired equipment, panels, or three-phase work — a narrow window. The load test draws real current and can trip sensitive circuits or upset connected electronics, so you have to think before testing live office or medical environments. Interpreting impedance and voltage drop results meaningfully takes understanding the instrument does not supply. It is expensive for its scope, and its GFCI trip testing can nuisance-trip some modern devices.
