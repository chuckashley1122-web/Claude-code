ServiceTitan: what it does and where it falls short

Built for residential service at volume, and it is the wrong purchase for a project-based electrical contractor.

Category
Software — All-in-one field service management
Starting price
Quote only — no public pricing
Best for
Residential electrical service companies with roughly ten or more field staff
Not a good fit for
Project and construction-led electrical contractors

What it does

ServiceTitan is a residential service operating system. Calls book on a screen showing the property's history, dispatch moves jobs on a live board, and the electrician arrives with a tablet that presents flat-rate options, documents findings with photos, collects payment and can submit a financing application on the spot. Panel upgrades, generator installations and whole-home surge or lighting work are exactly the kind of jobs its option-presentation and financing tooling is designed to sell.

The depth owners buy it for is in reporting and recurring revenue: revenue per electrician, average ticket, membership or service plan renewals, marketing spend attributed to the phone number that produced the call, plus job costing across labour and material. It also handles purchase orders, truck inventory, payroll timesheets and accounting sync.

Who it suits

This is for a residential service company managing people and margin.

  • Residential electrical service and replacement companies with real daily call volume.
  • Shops selling panel upgrades, generators and larger residential work where financing and option presentation move close rates.
  • Owners who want per-electrician performance data and will act on it.
  • Companies with someone who owns the software — pricebook maintenance, reporting, training.

Where it falls short

It is expensive, demanding, and specifically weak at the work many electrical contractors do most.

  • Project and construction work fits badly. Progress billing, AIA-style pay applications, retainage, change order management and certified payroll are not what it is built around.
  • Quote-only pricing at the top of the market, usually with an implementation fee. No way to compare it without a sales process.
  • Annual contracts that do not scale down. A slow quarter does not reduce the bill.
  • Implementation is a multi-week project centred on building a flat-rate pricebook. Skip that work and you have bought an expensive dispatch board.
  • Far heavier than a small shop needs — whole areas assume a dedicated dispatcher and CSR.

Before you buy

Split your revenue between residential service and contracted project work. If project work is more than a modest share, look at the construction-oriented platforms on this list instead.

Get the first-year total including implementation, the contract term and the renewal behaviour in writing before going further.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
