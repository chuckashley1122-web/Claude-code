Simpro: what it does and where it falls short

One of the few platforms that genuinely handles project work and recurring maintenance contracts in the same system.

Category
Software — Project and maintenance management for trades
Starting price
Quote only — no public pricing
Best for
Commercial electrical contractors running both projects and maintenance contracts
Not a good fit for
Small residential service shops

What it does

Simpro is built for commercial trades contracting. Estimating handles multi-section quotes with labour rates and material catalogues; projects are managed in stages with forecast versus actual cost, progress claims and variation orders; and job costing runs continuously so you can see a contract going wrong while there is still time to react rather than at final account.

The other half is recurring maintenance. Asset registers per site, planned maintenance schedules, service level agreements, and technician job completion against defined tasks — the pattern of a contractor holding testing, inspection or maintenance contracts on commercial buildings. Inventory, purchasing, subcontractor management and payroll and accounting integration are included.

Who it suits

This suits a commercial contractor with real project complexity.

  • Electrical contractors running fit-outs, installations or multi-stage projects where cost control decides profit.
  • Companies holding maintenance, testing or inspection contracts across multiple client sites.
  • Businesses that need forecast-versus-actual visibility during a job rather than after it.
  • Contractors with significant inventory and subcontractor management needs.

Where it falls short

It is powerful and correspondingly heavy.

  • Complexity is high. The learning curve is real for both office staff and field technicians, and adoption failures here are usually training failures.
  • Implementation is a project with a cost attached, and configuring rates, catalogues and workflows properly takes weeks.
  • No published pricing, so comparison requires a sales process.
  • Wrong tool for a small residential service shop — you would be buying and maintaining complexity you never use.
  • Its residential flat-rate selling, membership and consumer financing tooling is weak compared with the service-focused platforms.

Before you buy

Insist on a demo built around one of your own live projects, including a progress claim and a variation. Generic demos hide exactly the friction you need to see.

Ask what implementation costs, how long it takes, and who from your side has to be available during it.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
