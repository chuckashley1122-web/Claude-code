Klein Tools CL800: what it does and where it falls short

Covers the measurement a multimeter cannot — current without breaking the circuit — at a mid-range price.

Not a good fit for
Precision measurement work or very tight conductor access

What it does

A clamp meter measures current by sensing the magnetic field around a conductor, so you can read load current on a live circuit without disconnecting anything. The CL800 does AC and DC current, and adds the usual multimeter functions — voltage, resistance, continuity, capacitance, frequency, temperature with a thermocouple, and a low-impedance voltage mode that helps avoid false readings from induced ghost voltage on long runs.

It is a true-RMS instrument, which matters as much here as on a multimeter: measuring current on a distorted waveform with an averaging meter gives a wrong answer, and most modern loads are distorted. For diagnosing an overloaded circuit, checking motor running current or balancing a panel, this is the instrument that answers the question quickly.

Who it suits

A sensible everyday instrument for a service electrician.

  • Electricians checking load current, balancing panels or diagnosing nuisance tripping.
  • Service work where measuring motor and appliance running current is routine.
  • Technicians who want DC current capability for battery, solar or control circuits.
  • Anyone who wants one instrument covering current plus normal multimeter duties at a moderate price.

Where it falls short

It is a mid-price instrument and sits accordingly.

  • Accuracy is good for troubleshooting but below the premium industrial instruments. It is not the tool for measurements that have to be defensible.
  • Jaw size limits access. Crowded panels, large conductors and tightly bundled cables all defeat a clamp of this size.
  • Clamp measurement of low currents is inherently less accurate, so small loads and leakage current are outside what it does well.
  • Reading any current correctly requires clamping a single conductor. Clamp a cable containing both conductors and you read close to zero, which is a common and embarrassing error.
  • Build quality is solid rather than exceptional, and the included leads and thermocouple are basic.

Before you buy

Check the jaw opening against the conductors you most often need to measure. A clamp that will not fit around the cable is useless regardless of its specifications.

If you need to measure leakage or very small currents, that is a different instrument — a dedicated leakage clamp.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
