Fluke 87V: what it does and where it falls short

The long-standing default industrial multimeter, bought for accuracy and safety rating rather than features.

Starting price
Street price approx. $450–$550
Best for
Electricians who need one meter to cover service, industrial and motor drive work
Not a good fit for
Anyone needing only basic continuity and voltage checks

What it does

The 87V is a true-RMS digital multimeter aimed at industrial and commercial electrical work. It measures voltage, current, resistance, continuity, frequency, capacitance and temperature with a thermocouple, and its measurement accuracy is high enough to be trusted for calibration-adjacent work rather than just go/no-go checks.

Two features earn it its place. True-RMS measurement gives correct readings on distorted waveforms, which is most modern electrical work — an averaging meter simply reports the wrong number on a non-sinusoidal load. And a low-pass filter mode lets you read the fundamental output of a variable frequency drive without the switching noise producing nonsense. The CAT III 1000 V / CAT IV 600 V rating is the other reason it is specified: it is rated for the environments an industrial electrician actually works in.

Who it suits

This is a buy-once instrument for a working electrician.

  • Electricians doing a mix of commercial, industrial and service work who want one meter for all of it.
  • Anyone troubleshooting variable frequency drives or electronically controlled loads.
  • Work in higher-energy environments where the CAT rating matters for safety compliance, not marketing.
  • Technicians who need trustworthy accuracy rather than approximate readings.

Where it falls short

It is expensive, physically substantial, and more meter than many electricians need.

  • Several times the price of a competent basic multimeter. For residential service work that is mostly voltage presence and continuity, the extra capability goes unused.
  • Physically large and heavy for a handheld meter, which matters in a pouch all day.
  • Because it does so much, the function switch and menus take longer to navigate than a simpler meter on a repetitive task.
  • It is not a clamp meter. Measuring current still means breaking the circuit or carrying a second instrument.
  • Accuracy claims depend on calibration. A meter used hard for years without calibration is not the instrument the datasheet describes, and periodic calibration is a real recurring cost.

Before you buy

Be honest about whether you need true-RMS and the low-pass filter. If you do, buy this class of meter; if you are doing residential troubleshooting, a cheaper meter plus a clamp meter may serve better for the same money.

Budget for a calibration interval if your work requires documented measurement accuracy.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
