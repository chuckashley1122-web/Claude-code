Knowify: what it does and where it falls short

Built around the paperwork of commercial subcontracting — pay applications, change orders and certified payroll.

Category
Software — Commercial contracting and project accounting
Starting price
From approx. $150–$300/month depending on plan and users
Best for
Commercial electrical subcontractors doing contract and public works
Not a good fit for
Residential service businesses

What it does

Knowify targets the administrative reality of commercial subcontracting. Bids are built from labour and material takeoffs; awarded contracts are billed by schedule of values with AIA-style progress applications and retainage tracked properly; change orders are captured as documents that move through approval instead of verbal agreements nobody can evidence later; and certified payroll reports can be produced for public works jobs.

It sits closely alongside QuickBooks rather than replacing accounting, which is how most subcontractors of this size actually operate. Job costing runs against the contract so labour and material burn is visible against the schedule of values, and time tracking feeds both payroll and job cost from the same entry.

Who it suits

This is for a subcontractor whose problem is contract administration.

  • Commercial electrical subcontractors billing by schedule of values with retainage.
  • Companies doing public works or prevailing wage jobs requiring certified payroll.
  • Contractors losing money on unapproved or undocumented change orders.
  • Businesses running QuickBooks that want project accounting without migrating their books.

Where it falls short

It is a contracting and billing tool, not a service platform.

  • No real service dispatch capability. Same-day call handling, a live dispatch board and flat-rate residential selling are not what it does.
  • Field mobile functionality is more limited than the service-first platforms — it is strongest in the office.
  • It expects QuickBooks. If your accounting is elsewhere, much of the advantage evaporates.
  • Setup requires you to have your contract, billing and cost code structures in order first. It will not create discipline you do not have.
  • Inventory and purchasing depth is below the larger commercial platforms.

Before you buy

Confirm it handles your general contractors' specific billing formats and your state's certified payroll requirements, using a real job as the test.

If you run both service and contract work, plan for how the service side is handled before you commit.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
