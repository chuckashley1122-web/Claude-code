Fluke 1587 FC: what it does and where it falls short

Combines two instruments, which is convenient — and means compromises in both.

Category
Equipment — Insulation resistance multimeter

Best for
Electricians doing motor, cable and equipment insulation testing as well as general work
Not a good fit for
High-voltage cable testing or shops that rarely test insulation

What it does

The 1587 FC is an insulation resistance tester with a true-RMS digital multimeter built into the same body. Insulation testing at selectable voltages up to 1000 V lets you assess motor windings, cable runs and equipment insulation for degradation before it becomes a failure, with features including a timed test and a temperature-compensated result for comparable records over time.

The practical argument for a combined instrument is that insulation testing is rarely a standalone task. You measure voltage to prove the circuit is dead, test insulation, then measure again — and doing all of it with one tool instead of swapping instruments and leads speeds up a motor or cable investigation meaningfully. Wireless connectivity logs results to a phone for a record you can attach to a report.

Who it suits

This suits electricians for whom insulation testing is regular work.

  • Electricians maintaining motors, pumps and driven equipment where winding insulation is a known failure mode.
  • Contractors testing cable runs during installation or fault-finding.
  • Companies doing preventive maintenance who want temperature-corrected readings trended over time.
  • Technicians who want one instrument instead of a meter plus a separate insulation tester.

Where it falls short

Combined instruments always compromise, and this one is expensive.

  • Roughly double the price of a good standalone multimeter, so if you seldom test insulation the capability is expensive idle weight.
  • 1000 V maximum test voltage is fine for most low-voltage work but not enough for medium or high-voltage cable testing — that needs a dedicated higher-voltage tester.
  • As a combined tool it is bulkier than a plain multimeter, and it is a single point of failure: send it for repair or calibration and you lose both functions.
  • Insulation results need interpretation. A raw resistance figure means little without temperature correction, trend history and knowledge of the equipment — the instrument will not tell you whether a reading is acceptable.
  • Insulation testing applies a high voltage and can damage connected electronics. Proper isolation and disconnection procedure is entirely on the user.

Before you buy

Count how often you actually perform insulation tests. If it is occasional, a separate tester borrowed or shared across the company may be the better spend.

Confirm the test voltages your work requires. If anything you do needs above 1000 V, this is not the instrument.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
