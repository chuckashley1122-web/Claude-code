Housecall Pro: what it does and where it falls short

The usual first step off paper and spreadsheets for a small HVAC shop, and easy to get running in a day.

Category
Software — Field service management for small teams
Starting price
From approx. $79/month (published tiers)
Best for
One to roughly ten technicians wanting to get off paper quickly
Not a good fit for
Commercial contractors with complex project billing

What it does

Housecall Pro handles the everyday loop of a small service business: book the job, put it on a calendar, send the customer an automatic reminder, have the technician arrive with the job details on a phone, build an estimate or invoice on site, and take a card payment before leaving. Automated follow-up messages and review requests are built in, as are recurring service plans for maintenance agreements.

It also covers the surrounding admin: a customer database with equipment and job history, price book entries you can drop into estimates, technician GPS and time tracking, QuickBooks sync, and a reporting section covering revenue, job types and technician output. Most of it is usable from the phone app, which is the point — it assumes the person entering data is standing in a mechanical room.

Who it suits

This is a strong fit for a shop whose current system is a paper calendar, a spreadsheet, or text messages.

  • Owner-operators and small crews who need scheduling, invoicing and card payments working this week, not next quarter.
  • Residential service and maintenance work with short, repeatable jobs.
  • Shops that want published pricing they can evaluate without sitting through a sales call.
  • Teams where the owner is still in the field and will be using the app themselves.

Where it falls short

Its strength is how fast it gets going; the limits show up once a company has scale or complexity.

  • Reporting and job costing are thinner than the larger platforms. You can see revenue and job counts easily, but true per-job profitability with labour burden and overhead takes work outside the tool.
  • Features are split across tiers and add-ons, so the headline price is rarely the price. Things a growing shop wants — more seats, advanced reporting, sales proposal tools — push you up the pricing ladder.
  • Commercial work fits awkwardly. Progress billing, retainage, multi-phase projects and complicated PO requirements are not what it was designed around.
  • Inventory and purchasing are basic. Shops tracking stock across multiple vans and a warehouse usually keep a separate system.
  • Payment processing fees are part of the real cost. Compare the effective rate against your existing merchant account rather than assuming the convenience is free.

Before you buy

Price out the tier that actually contains the features you listed as requirements, at your real seat count, and add expected card processing volume. That number is the comparison figure — not the entry price.

Trial it during a busy week rather than a quiet one. The questions worth answering are whether your least technically-inclined technician will use the app, and whether dispatch holds up when three calls move at once.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
