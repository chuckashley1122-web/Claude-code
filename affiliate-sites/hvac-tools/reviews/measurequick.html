What it does

measureQuick pulls readings from supported wireless probes and gauges into one live picture of a system — superheat, subcooling, airflow, static pressure, delivered capacity — then grades the installation against manufacturer targets and flags which fault is most likely. It produces a customer-ready report showing what was measured before and after the work.

Who it suits

Companies competing on quality rather than price, technicians who want to stop guessing at airflow, and owners trying to standardize how every tech diagnoses a system. It is also a strong training tool, because it makes bad readings visibly bad instead of leaving them to interpretation.

Where it falls short

It is only as good as the instruments feeding it, so the real cost is the probe kit, not the subscription — budget for hardware before the software. The learning curve is steeper than the marketing suggests, and techs who do not understand the underlying psychrometrics will misread its conclusions with new confidence. Bluetooth connection management across a pile of probes is genuinely fiddly in the field. Older or unusual equipment sometimes lacks the manufacturer data needed for meaningful grading.
