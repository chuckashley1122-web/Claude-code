measureQuick: what it does and where it falls short

Turns a set of instrument readings into a verdict on how the system is actually performing, and documents it.

Category
Software — System performance diagnostics app
Starting price
Free tier; paid plans from approx. $15–$40/month
Best for
Technicians and shops committed to measured, documented diagnostics
Not a good fit for
Shops that will not invest in probes and training

What it does

measureQuick connects to Bluetooth probes — manifolds, psychrometers, clamp meters, anemometers, combustion analysers — from several manufacturers, collects the readings in one place, and interprets them. Instead of leaving a technician to work out what a suction pressure and a wet-bulb reading mean together, it calculates delivered capacity, airflow, static pressure health and charge status, and flags which fault is most probable.

The output is a report. A completed test produces before-and-after documentation showing what the system was doing, what was changed, and what it does now — usable with a homeowner, a warranty claim, or a commissioning record. For shops trying to move from parts-swapping to measured diagnostics, that documented result is the main product.

Who it suits

This is for technicians who want to prove what a system is doing, not estimate it.

  • Shops doing commissioning, performance verification or quality-assurance checks on installs.
  • Technicians who already own wireless probes and want more from them than a digital gauge readout.
  • Companies whose sales approach depends on showing a homeowner measured evidence rather than an opinion.
  • Trainers and service managers using recorded tests to review technician work.

Where it falls short

The software is only as good as the instruments and the technician feeding it.

  • It needs compatible wireless probes to be useful. The real cost of adoption is the instrument kit per technician, which dwarfs the subscription.
  • There is a genuine learning curve. Technicians who do not understand the underlying psychrometrics can misread the guidance, and the app will confidently analyse bad inputs.
  • Test sequences take longer than a quick gauge-and-go check. On short service calls, technicians skip it unless the shop insists.
  • Bluetooth connectivity across several probes is fiddly, and dropouts mid-test are an ordinary annoyance.
  • It diagnoses and documents; it does not schedule, invoice or dispatch. It sits alongside your office platform, not instead of it.

Before you buy

Cost it per technician including the probes you would need, not as a monthly subscription. That figure is what you are really deciding on.

Plan the training. Shops that hand it out without teaching the fundamentals get expensive probes and unused software.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
