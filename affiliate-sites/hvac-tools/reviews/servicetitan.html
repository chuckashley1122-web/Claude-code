ServiceTitan: what it does and where it falls short

The most complete HVAC office platform on this list, and the most demanding to own.

Category
Software — All-in-one field service management
Starting price
Quote only — no public pricing
Best for
Established residential companies, roughly 10 trucks and up, with an office team to run it
Not a good fit for
Owner-operators and two-truck shops

What it does

ServiceTitan is an end-to-end operating system for a residential service company. Calls come in through a booking screen that shows the customer's equipment and service history, jobs land on a dispatch board, and technicians work from a tablet app that walks them through a pricebook, builds good-better-best options, captures photos and takes payment or a financing application in the driveway.

Behind that sits the part companies actually buy it for: reporting. Revenue per technician, average ticket, membership renewal rates, marketing spend attributed down to the phone number that rang, and job costing that accounts for labour and materials. It also handles maintenance agreements, inventory, purchase orders, payroll timesheets and an accounting sync.

Who it suits

ServiceTitan is built for a company that has outgrown a scheduling tool and now needs to manage a business.

  • Residential HVAC and plumbing companies with enough call volume that dispatch efficiency is a real cost line.
  • Owners who want per-technician performance numbers and marketing attribution, and will act on them.
  • Shops selling memberships and equipment replacements, where in-app financing and option presentation change close rates.
  • Companies with at least one person whose job includes owning the software — keeping the pricebook current, building reports, training new hires.

Where it falls short

The gap between what ServiceTitan can do and what a given company gets out of it is usually the problem, not the software itself.

  • Pricing is quote-only and, by every public account, the most expensive option in this category. There is typically an implementation fee on top of the recurring cost, and you cannot compare it against alternatives without going through a sales process.
  • Contracts are typically annual and the cost does not scale down gracefully if you shrink — a seasonal dip or losing a couple of technicians does not reduce the bill mid-term.
  • Implementation is a project, not a weekend. Pricebook build, data migration and technician training take weeks, and shops that skip the pricebook work end up with an expensive dispatch board.
  • It is heavier than a small shop needs. Many screens exist for roles — dedicated dispatcher, CSR, accounting — that an owner-operator doesn't have, and the extra clicks are real.
  • Configuration depth cuts both ways: it is flexible, which also means a lot of decisions are yours to get right, and undoing a bad early setup is painful.

Before you buy

Ask specifically about the total first-year figure including implementation, the contract term, what happens to your rate at renewal, and how many seats of each type are included. Those four answers determine whether the platform is viable for you far more than any feature list.

It is also worth asking who on your team will own the pricebook. If the honest answer is nobody, a lighter platform will serve you better.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
