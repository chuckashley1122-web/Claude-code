FLIR C5: what it does and where it falls short

Thermal imaging at a price and size that makes it a pocket tool rather than a special trip to the van.

Category
Equipment — Pocket thermal imaging camera
Starting price
Street price approx. $550–$700
Best for
Technicians and estimators who want thermal imaging they will actually carry
Not a good fit for
Quantitative building diagnostics or energy audit reporting

What it does

The C5 is a compact thermal camera roughly the size of a phone. In HVAC work it earns its place on the diagnostic and sales side: finding duct leakage and disconnected runs, showing missing or compressed insulation, spotting wet insulation and water intrusion, checking radiant floor loops, and catching overheating contactors and loose electrical connections before they fail.

It blends thermal data with a visible-light image, which matters more than it sounds — a raw low-resolution thermal frame is hard to explain to a homeowner, while a blended image showing a cold stripe across a recognisable ceiling is immediately legible. Images upload to a cloud account for attaching to reports and estimates.

Who it suits

This is the thermal camera you buy because you will have it with you.

  • Service technicians doing comfort complaints, where showing the problem shortens the conversation.
  • Estimators and comfort advisors documenting insulation and duct issues during a home assessment.
  • Anyone doing preventive electrical checks on panels, contactors and motor terminals.
  • Shops that want thermal imaging on several trucks rather than one expensive camera in the office.

Where it falls short

It is an entry-level camera, and the limits are real ones.

  • Thermal resolution is low. It shows you where to look; it will not resolve small or distant detail, and fine differences get lost.
  • Temperature measurement accuracy is fine for comparison but not for quantitative work — emissivity and reflection errors bite, and it is not the tool for formal energy audit numbers.
  • Thermal images are easy to misread. Reflections, surface materials and recent sun exposure all produce convincing patterns that mean nothing, and a confidently wrong reading to a homeowner is a real risk.
  • No interchangeable lens and a fixed field of view, so you cannot adapt it to tight or distant targets.
  • Battery is internal and non-swappable, which limits a long day of imaging.

Before you buy

Be clear about the job. If you want a camera to point at things and find anomalies, this is well matched. If you need defensible temperature measurements or high-resolution reporting, budget for a higher class of camera instead.

Spend an afternoon learning how reflection and emissivity mislead before showing images to customers.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
