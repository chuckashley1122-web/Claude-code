ServiceTrade: what it does and where it falls short

Built for commercial contract work, where the deliverable is a documented inspection rather than a repaired furnace.

Category
Software — Commercial service management
Starting price
Quote only — no public pricing
Best for
Commercial mechanical contractors doing contracted maintenance and inspections
Not a good fit for
Residential demand-service companies

What it does

ServiceTrade organises work the way a commercial maintenance contract does: a customer with multiple buildings, each with an asset list, each asset on a recurring inspection schedule. Technicians complete inspections on a tablet against defined checklists, log deficiencies with photos, and the platform turns those deficiencies into quotable repair work rather than notes that die in a filing cabinet.

Its other emphasis is customer-facing documentation. Job records with photos, readings and technician notes can be shared with the building owner, which is how commercial contractors justify invoices and renew contracts. It also covers planned maintenance scheduling across a year, quoting, purchasing and accounting integration.

Who it suits

This is a commercial tool and makes little sense outside that context.

  • Mechanical contractors whose revenue is contracted maintenance and inspection rather than break-fix calls.
  • Companies managing asset lists across many buildings per customer.
  • Operations where converting logged deficiencies into approved repair quotes is a main growth lever.
  • Contractors who need to hand a property manager clean, photo-backed documentation of every visit.

Where it falls short

The commercial focus is genuine, which means the residential side is not served.

  • Residential demand service is a poor fit. Same-day dispatch, flat-rate option selling, homeowner financing and membership programmes are not what it is shaped around.
  • No public pricing, and the cost structure assumes a commercial contractor's budget.
  • Setup is front-loaded and heavy: the value depends on building accurate asset lists per site, which is real fieldwork before the software helps anyone.
  • For a mixed shop, running residential work through it alongside commercial contracts usually means compromises or a second system.
  • Technician-facing screens prioritise thorough data capture over speed, which technicians on short calls notice.

Before you buy

Be honest about your revenue split. If more than a modest share of your work is residential break-fix, this will fight you.

Ask how asset data gets loaded and who does it. Whoever owns that task determines when the platform starts paying back.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
