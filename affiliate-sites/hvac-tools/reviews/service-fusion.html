What it does

Service Fusion bundles customer management, estimating, dispatch, job costing, invoicing, and QuickBooks synchronization, and prices by tier rather than per user. That flat-rate structure is the central pitch: a shop with a lot of occasional users — subs, part-timers, office staff who log in twice a week — is not penalized for adding accounts. GPS fleet tracking is available as a paid add-on.

Who it suits

Companies with a wide, shallow user base where per-seat pricing on competing platforms becomes the dominant cost, and shops that live in QuickBooks and want two-way sync rather than a separate accounting universe.

Where it falls short

The interface looks and feels dated next to newer platforms, and that shows up as real friction in training and daily use. The mobile app is the weakest part of the product — techs report sync lag and lost entries on poor connections, which is exactly when a field app needs to be reliable. Reporting is serviceable but rigid, with limited custom report building. The flat-rate advantage also shrinks fast if you need the add-ons, since fleet tracking and some integrations are separately billed.
