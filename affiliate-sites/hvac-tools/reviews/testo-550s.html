Testo 550s: what it does and where it falls short

Smaller and tidier than most digital manifolds, with guided test routines that reduce judgement calls.

Category
Equipment — Two-valve wireless digital manifold
Starting price
Street price approx. $400–$600 depending on kit
Best for
Technicians who want a compact, well-built manifold with guided routines
Not a good fit for
Work needing four ports for recovery and charging simultaneously

What it does

The 550s is a two-valve digital manifold aimed at everyday charging and evacuation. It reads pressures, calculates superheat and subcooling using wireless temperature clamps instead of tethered leads, carries a refrigerant library, and measures vacuum. Build quality and display legibility are consistent strengths of the range.

Its distinguishing feature is guided routines. Evacuation and tightness tests run as automated sequences — the instrument watches the decay and tells you whether the system passed, rather than leaving the technician to interpret a drifting number. Results sync to a phone app that stores measurements and generates a job report.

Who it suits

A good fit for technicians who value a compact, durable instrument over maximum port count.

  • Residential service technicians doing routine charging and evacuation work.
  • Anyone who dislikes tangled thermocouple leads — the wireless clamps are a genuine improvement in a cramped space.
  • Newer technicians who benefit from automated pass/fail evacuation and tightness routines.
  • Shops wanting documented readings without a heavy four-port manifold on every truck.

Where it falls short

The compact design has consequences, and the accessory ecosystem adds up.

  • Two ports only. Jobs where you want recovery and charging paths connected at once need a four-valve manifold or extra plumbing.
  • The wireless temperature clamps are the good part and are often sold as a kit or separately — the base instrument price is not the working price.
  • More separate wireless pieces means more batteries and more things to lose out of the case.
  • The app is functional but less open than some competitors, so check integration if you rely on a third-party diagnostics platform.
  • Vacuum measurement is present but, as with any manifold-mounted sensor, a dedicated micron gauge at the system is still better practice on critical evacuations.

Before you buy

Price the kit you would actually carry — instrument plus the number of temperature clamps you need — rather than the base unit.

If you frequently work jobs needing four ports, size up rather than buying this and adding fittings to compensate.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
