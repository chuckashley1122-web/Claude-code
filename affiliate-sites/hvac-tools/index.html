HVAC contractor software and equipment, compared

This site covers ten things an HVAC contractor is likely to be shopping for: the office software that runs dispatch and invoicing, and the instruments a technician carries into an attic. They are grouped together because the buying decision is usually the same one — you have money to spend on making the work go faster, and you want to know where it goes furthest.

Each tool below has its own page covering what it does, the kind of shop it suits, and a section on where it falls short. That last part is the reason this site exists: almost every product here is good at something and frustrating at something else, and the frustrating part is what you find out in month three.

How this list is put together

The list is built from vendor documentation, published pricing pages, product manuals and specification sheets. Where a vendor does not publish a price, the table says so rather than guessing at a number.

Tools were included because they come up repeatedly in HVAC work, cover a spread of company sizes, or fill a job nothing else on the list does. The mix is deliberate:

  • Five office platforms, from one-truck operations to commercial mechanical contractors.
  • One diagnostics app that ties wireless probes to a system-performance reading.
  • Two digital manifolds, because that is the instrument a technician touches on nearly every call.
  • One thermal camera and one recovery machine, as examples of the gear that pays for itself on specific job types rather than every job.
  • No product was included or excluded because of an affiliate relationship.

What we do not publish

You will not find scores, star ratings, review counts, testimonials, awards or income claims anywhere on this site. We have no way to verify those things for the products listed here, so we leave them out rather than make them up. Every write-up includes a plain “where it falls short” section instead.
