What it does

The ColorQ Pro 7 is a handheld photometer that reads seven parameters — free and total chlorine, bromine, pH, alkalinity, calcium hardness, and cyanuric acid — and reports a number on a screen instead of asking you to match a color. Removing human color judgment means two techs on the same water get the same answer, which is the operational argument for it.

Who it suits

Route technicians who need defensible numbers quickly, companies standardizing testing across multiple techs, and anyone whose color vision or working conditions make visual comparators unreliable.

Where it falls short

It still uses consumable reagents, so the running cost does not go away, and tablet or liquid reagents must be kept dry and in date. Accuracy depends on clean, unscratched sample tubes — cloudy or scratched vials produce quietly wrong numbers. It is an electronic device on a wet pool deck, and drops kill it. High chlorine levels can read out of range and require dilution, reintroducing the error you bought it to avoid. Calibration drift over time needs checking against a wet kit, which means you should own one anyway.
