Pool Brain: what it does and where it falls short

Aimed at the two things that break as a route business grows: dosing accuracy and knowing what technicians actually did.

What it does

Pool Brain's emphasis is chemistry and accountability. It holds a detailed profile per pool — volume, surface, sanitiser type, equipment — and calculates required dosing from the readings a technician enters, rather than relying on the technician's own arithmetic. Over time it builds a chemistry history per pool that shows which pools are persistently off and why.

The accountability side is the other half. It checks whether the readings and work logged at a stop are plausible and complete, flags stops that look skipped or rushed, and gives an owner a view of technician consistency rather than a list of completed jobs. Routes, service reports to customers and recurring billing are all covered as well.

Who it suits

This suits an owner who no longer sees every pool personally.

  • Companies with several technicians where dosing mistakes or skipped stops are causing callbacks.
  • Owners who want chemistry decisions taken out of individual technicians' judgement.
  • Businesses with problem pools that need a chemistry history to diagnose.
  • Operations training newer technicians who benefit from being told what to add rather than working it out.

Where it falls short

The depth that makes it useful is also what makes it work to set up.

  • Setup is heavier than route-only software. Accurate dosing depends on accurate pool profiles — volumes, surfaces, equipment — and gathering that for an existing customer base is real work before the system helps.
  • Smaller and less established than the market leader, which means fewer integrations and a smaller pool of people who know it.
  • Solo operators are paying for oversight features they have no use for.
  • Automated dosing is only as good as the reading entered. It will confidently calculate a dose from a bad test, so testing discipline still has to be enforced.
  • Repair and construction job management is limited, as with most pool-specific platforms.

Before you buy

Be realistic about collecting pool profile data. If nobody will measure volumes and record equipment for your existing customers, the dosing engine will not deliver.

Compare it against the simpler route platforms honestly — if your problem is billing rather than chemistry, buy the simpler thing.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
