Skimmer: what it does and where it falls short

The best-known pool-specific platform, and built around the route rather than adapted to it.

Category
Software — Pool route and service management
Starting price
From approx. $59/month, scaling with stop count
Best for
Route-based cleaning companies of almost any size
Not a good fit for
Companies whose main business is construction and renovation

What it does

Skimmer is organised around the service stop. A technician opens the day's route, works down it, and at each pool records the readings taken, the chemicals dosed, the tasks completed and photos of the work. That record turns into a service report emailed to the customer automatically, which is the single biggest reason companies adopt it — customers stop asking whether anyone came.

Around that sit the business parts: route building and optimisation, technician assignment and route swaps when someone is out, chemical usage tracking so you can see what each pool is actually costing you, recurring invoicing with automatic card and bank payment collection, and reporting on route profitability. Repairs and one-off work orders are supported alongside the routes.

Who it suits

This is the default recommendation for a cleaning route business.

  • Companies whose revenue is recurring weekly or biweekly service stops.
  • Owners losing customers to the perception that service is being skipped — the photo service report solves that directly.
  • Shops that want chemical cost tracked per pool rather than as one lump expense.
  • Businesses wanting recurring billing that collects itself instead of monthly invoice chasing.

Where it falls short

It is excellent at routes, and thinner everywhere else.

  • Repair and renovation workflow is lighter than a general field service platform. Multi-day jobs, parts ordering and detailed job costing on a big equipment replacement are not its strength.
  • Pricing scales with stop count, so a large route base makes it a substantial monthly cost — check the tier pricing at your real stop count rather than the entry figure.
  • Retail counter operations, inventory across a storefront and point of sale are outside its scope entirely.
  • Technician adoption is the whole game. A technician who doesn't log readings and photos produces an empty service report, which is worse than not promising one.
  • Reporting is good on routes and chemicals but does not reach the depth of a full business analytics platform.

Before you buy

Get the price at your actual stop count and compare it against the value of the billing you currently fail to collect on time. For most route businesses that comparison is the whole case.

Decide what share of your revenue is repair work. If it is large, plan on a second system or accept the limits.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
