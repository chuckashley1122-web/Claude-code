LaMotte WaterLink Spin Touch: what it does and where it falls short

Removes human judgement from testing entirely, at a capital cost and a per-test cost.

Category
Equipment — Photometric water testing system
Starting price
Approx. $1,500–$2,200 for the unit, plus reagent discs per test
Best for
Retail counters and multi-technician companies wanting fast, consistent full panels
Not a good fit for
Small operators — the per-test cost and capital outlay do not fit

What it does

The Spin Touch is a photometer. A small water sample is injected into a sealed reagent disc, the unit spins the disc to distribute the sample into pre-measured reagent chambers, and it reads the resulting colours optically — producing a full panel of results in roughly a minute with no drop counting and no colour judgement by eye.

Two consequences matter commercially. First, consistency: every technician and every counter staff member produces the same reading from the same water, which removes a common source of arguments and misdosing. Second, documentation: results log digitally and feed software that generates treatment recommendations and a printed report, which is standard practice at a retail counter and increasingly expected in service.

Who it suits

This suits operations where testing volume and consistency justify the outlay.

  • Retail pool stores running water tests as a footfall and sales driver.
  • Multi-technician service companies wanting identical results regardless of who tested.
  • Companies servicing commercial water where documented full panels are required.
  • Businesses that want test results logged into software automatically rather than transcribed.

Where it falls short

The economics are the main obstacle, not the technology.

  • High capital cost, and reagent discs are priced per test. A route technician testing dozens of pools a week generates a significant consumable bill.
  • Discs have a shelf life and are sensitive to heat and humidity, so bulk buying and truck storage both need care.
  • It is a piece of electronics with a fluid path. It needs cleaning, occasional calibration checks and careful handling, and a field failure leaves you with no testing capability.
  • Sample handling still matters. A poor sample or a contaminated syringe produces a precise, confident, wrong answer.
  • For small operators the maths rarely works — a reagent kit costs a fraction of this and, used properly, is at least as accurate.

Before you buy

Calculate your cost per test at real volume, including discs, and compare against reagent testing. Then decide whether consistency and speed are worth the difference.

If you buy one, keep a reagent kit as a backup. A dead photometer on a Saturday should not stop you working.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
