Pentair IntelliChlor IC40: what it does and where it falls short

Generates chlorine continuously, which changes the service visit — and introduces a cell that will need replacing.

Category
Equipment — Salt chlorine generator cell
Starting price
Cell street price approx. $800–$1,300; full system with power centre higher
Best for
Residential pools converting from manual chlorine dosing
Not a good fit for
Pools where salt is unsuitable, or owners unwilling to replace the cell periodically

What it does

A salt chlorine generator passes pool water containing dissolved salt over electrically charged plates, converting chloride into chlorine in the plumbing line. The pool is sanitised continuously while the pump runs, rather than in steps when someone adds liquid or tablets, which produces steadier chlorine levels and avoids the cyanuric acid accumulation that comes with stabilised tablets.

The IC40 is the mid-large residential size in the range, rated for pools up to roughly 40,000 gallons, with an on-board display showing salt level, water temperature and cell condition. For a service company, salt pools generally mean less chemical carrying and more stable water between visits — but they also mean a piece of equipment with a definite service life sitting in the customer's plumbing.

Who it suits

A reasonable recommendation for the right residential pool and the right owner.

  • Residential pools where the owner wants steadier chlorine and less manual dosing.
  • Pools suffering cyanuric acid build-up from continuous tablet use.
  • Customers on an existing Pentair automation system, where it integrates cleanly.
  • Service companies wanting more predictable water between visits on a difficult route.

Where it falls short

Salt systems are not maintenance-free, and the recurring cost surprises owners.

  • The cell is a wear item with a finite life measured in a few years, and replacement cells are expensive. An owner who was not told this will be angry at you, not at Pentair.
  • Cells scale in hard water and need periodic inspection and cleaning. Neglected cells lose output and fail early.
  • Salt water is harder on some pool and deck materials, on nearby metal fixtures and on certain stone, and it accelerates corrosion where equipment is not rated for it.
  • Output falls significantly in cold water, so in cooler seasons the pool may need supplementary chlorine anyway.
  • It does not eliminate water chemistry work. pH tends to rise in salt pools and needs managing, and stabiliser, alkalinity and calcium still require attention.

Before you buy

Tell the customer the cell replacement cost and expected life in writing before installation. Almost every complaint about salt systems traces to this conversation not happening.

Check the pool's surrounding materials, metal fixtures and existing equipment for salt compatibility before recommending a conversion.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
