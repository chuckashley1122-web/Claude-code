Taylor K-2006: what it does and where it falls short

The accuracy benchmark for field testing, and slower than everything else for exactly that reason.

Category
Equipment — Reagent water test kit
Starting price
Approx. $80–$120 for the kit; reagents replaced periodically
Best for
Technicians who need accurate chlorine and combined chlorine readings
Not a good fit for
Technicians who will not spend the time to titrate properly

What it does

The K-2006 is a reagent test kit built around FAS-DPD titration for chlorine. Rather than comparing a colour to a printed block, you add reagent drop by drop and count the drops to a colour change, which gives a numeric result with far better resolution — and, critically, lets you measure combined chlorine separately from free chlorine. That distinction is what tells you whether a pool has a chloramine problem, which a colour-match strip or comparator cannot reliably do.

The kit also covers pH, total alkalinity, calcium hardness and cyanuric acid, which together are the numbers you need to make a real balancing decision rather than a guess. For a service technician diagnosing a persistently problematic pool, it is the difference between knowing what is wrong and adding chemicals hopefully.

Who it suits

This is for the technician who wants the real number.

  • Diagnosing problem pools where combined chlorine is the likely cause.
  • Companies that want defensible readings when a customer disputes water quality.
  • Technicians handling high-bather-load or commercial water where accuracy is not optional.
  • Anyone doing chemistry calculations that depend on accurate cyanuric acid and calcium hardness numbers.

Where it falls short

Accuracy costs time, and reagents do not last forever.

  • A full test takes several minutes of careful drop counting. On a dense route, technicians quietly stop doing it and go back to strips.
  • Reagents have a limited shelf life and degrade with heat. A kit that lives in a hot truck all summer will give you wrong answers confidently.
  • It requires technique. Miscounting drops, dirty sample tubes or a sloppy sample all produce bad results, and the kit gives no warning.
  • Ongoing reagent cost is real and recurring, especially across several technicians.
  • Glass and liquid reagents in a service bag are fragile and messy, and spills in a truck are unpleasant.

Before you buy

Decide when it is required rather than making it every stop. Many companies use fast testing routinely and the titration kit on problem pools and monthly full tests, which is a workable compromise.

Buy reagents in quantities you will use before they expire, and store them out of the heat.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
