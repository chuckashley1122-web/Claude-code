SumoQuote: what it does and where it falls short

A proposal tool, not a CRM — bought to make the document you hand a homeowner do more of the selling.

Category
Software — Roofing proposal and quote presentation
Starting price
From approx. $99/month (published tiers, typically per company with user limits)
Best for
Sales-led roofing companies where proposal quality affects close rate
Not a good fit for
Companies whose CRM already produces proposals they are happy with

What it does

SumoQuote turns a price into a presentation. A proposal is assembled from templates: good-better-best options the homeowner can compare, photos from the inspection, explanations of the products and the scope, warranty and workmanship detail, financing information, and an e-signature block so the job can be accepted on the spot.

It is deliberately narrow. It does not run your pipeline or your production; it connects to CRMs and measurement services so the data arrives automatically and you spend your time on presentation rather than typing. The premise is that roofing is often won on clarity and trust, and a one-page price on a carbon-copy form loses to a document that explains itself.

Who it suits

This is for companies where the proposal is part of the sale.

  • Sales-led residential roofers presenting options in the home.
  • Companies whose current quotes are plain documents and who lose to better-presented competitors.
  • Shops wanting consistent proposals across several reps rather than each rep's own format.
  • Teams that use a CRM it integrates with, so measurements and customer data flow in.

Where it falls short

Narrow scope is intentional, but it has to be worth a separate subscription.

  • It only produces proposals. Every other part of running the job is somewhere else.
  • It overlaps with proposal features your CRM may already have, so you can end up paying twice for the same job.
  • Stacked on a CRM, a measurement service and a photo app, the combined monthly software bill gets large for a small company.
  • Template building takes real effort up front, and the output is only as good as the templates and photos your reps put into it.
  • A polished proposal does not fix pricing, follow-up or a weak pitch — it will not rescue a broken sales process.

Before you buy

Look hard at what your existing CRM's proposal output can be made to do first. If the gap is presentation polish rather than capability, that is a cheaper fix.

Total up your whole software stack monthly before adding this. The decision is about the stack, not the line item.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
