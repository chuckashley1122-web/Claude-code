What it does

A pneumatic coil nailer built for asphalt shingles, taking 7/8-inch to 1-3/4-inch coil roofing nails with tool-free depth adjustment, a side-load canister, and a carbide insert at the nose to slow wear. It is a long-standing crew standard on residential shingle work for straightforward reasons: it is light, it drives consistently, and it keeps running.

Who it suits

Production shingle crews and contractors who already run a compressor and hoses. For anyone nailing off a full roof in a day, the weight and balance matter more than any spec on the box.

Where it falls short

It is pneumatic, so the real cost includes a compressor, hose, and fittings, and you are managing hoses on a roof all day — cordless options now exist and eliminate that entirely. Depth consistency drifts as the nose wears and needs periodic readjustment. It is built for asphalt shingles and is the wrong tool for tile, metal, or low-slope systems. Jams happen with off-brand or slightly corroded coil nails, so nail quality is not the place to economize. Daily oiling is required and skipping it shortens its life quickly.
