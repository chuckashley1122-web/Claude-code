Hover: what it does and where it falls short

Measurements plus a picture of the finished house, which is a selling tool as much as an estimating one.

Category
Service — Photo-based 3D measurement and visualisation
Starting price
Priced per property — commonly from approx. $25 per property depending on plan and detail level
Best for
Exterior contractors who sell in the home and want visualisation as part of the pitch
Not a good fit for
Estimating properties you cannot visit

What it does

Hover works from photographs rather than aerial imagery. Someone walks the property taking a guided set of smartphone photos, and Hover returns a dimensioned 3D model of the house — roof measurements, but also siding areas, window and door counts, trim lengths and openings, which matters for contractors selling more than a roof.

The part that changes sales conversations is visualisation. Because there is a model of the actual house, you can render shingle colours, siding products and trim choices onto it and show a homeowner their own home finished, rather than a sample board. Measurements export into estimating and CRM tools the same way aerial reports do.

Who it suits

Hover fits a sales process that happens at the house.

  • Exterior contractors selling roofing alongside siding, windows or trim, where whole-house measurements are needed.
  • In-home sales teams who close better with a visual of the finished product.
  • Companies whose reps are already on site and can spend ten minutes capturing photos.
  • Shops wanting measurements for surfaces aerial imagery cannot see.

Where it falls short

The photo-based approach is the strength and the constraint.

  • Someone has to be at the property. You cannot estimate remotely, which rules out a workflow built on quoting from the office.
  • Output quality depends on capture quality. A rushed or incomplete photo set produces a poor model, and the person capturing is often your least patient rep.
  • Tight lots, fences, dense landscaping and adjoining buildings make it hard to get the angles the model needs.
  • Priced per property, so like any per-job service the cost of unsold estimates is a real line item.
  • Visualisation renders are a sales aid, not a colour-accurate promise — manage expectations or you will be arguing about shade after installation.

Before you buy

Train whoever captures photos properly and check the first several models. Most complaints about photo-based measurement trace back to the capture, not the service.

If you also want office-based remote estimating, plan on using an aerial service alongside it rather than expecting one tool to cover both.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
