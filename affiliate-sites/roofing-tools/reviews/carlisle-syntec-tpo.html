Carlisle SynTec TPO: what it does and where it falls short

The standard answer for a low-slope commercial roof, provided your crew can weld it properly.

Material — Commercial single-ply roofing membrane

Material only, commonly approx. $1.50–$3.50 per square foot depending on thickness and system

Commercial low-slope re-roofs and new construction

Residential steep-slope work, or crews without welding training

What it does

TPO is a thermoplastic single-ply membrane installed over a low-slope deck and seamed by heat welding, which produces a seam that is effectively part of the sheet rather than an adhesive joint. It is offered in multiple thicknesses, in mechanically attached, fully adhered and ballasted configurations, and most commonly in white — the reflective surface reduces cooling load and roof surface temperature, which is a real operating-cost argument for a building owner.

Carlisle sells it as a complete assembly: insulation, cover board, fasteners, adhesives, flashings and accessories, with system warranties available through authorised applicators. For a commercial contractor, that assembly approach is the product — the membrane on its own is only part of what gets warranted and inspected.

Who it suits

This is commercial low-slope work and expects a trained crew.

  • Commercial re-roofs and new low-slope construction where a reflective membrane suits the climate and the owner's energy position.
  • Contractors authorised to install the system and offer its warranties.
  • Projects where a welded seam is preferable to adhered laps in the long term.
  • Buildings where roof surface temperature and cooling load are part of the specification.

Where it falls short

Almost every TPO problem traces back to installation rather than the sheet.

  • It lives and dies on welding quality. Welds need the right temperature, speed and pressure for the conditions, and they need probing — an untrained crew will produce seams that look fine and fail in a year.
  • Weather sensitivity during installation is significant. Cold, wind, damp surfaces and dust all affect weld quality, which compresses the working window.
  • System warranties require authorised applicator status and inspection, and specify the full assembly. Substituting a cheaper insulation or fastener can void the coverage you quoted.
  • White membranes show dirt, and a soiled reflective roof loses some of the energy benefit that justified it.
  • Detailing around penetrations, curbs and drains is where low-slope roofs leak, and there are a lot of details on a typical commercial roof. Material choice does not reduce that workload.

Before you buy

Confirm your crew's welding training and your probing procedure before bidding. If you cannot test seams properly, this is the wrong system to learn on.

Get the warranty requirements in writing — which components, which thickness, which attachment — and price the assembly the warranty actually requires, not a cheaper version of it.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
