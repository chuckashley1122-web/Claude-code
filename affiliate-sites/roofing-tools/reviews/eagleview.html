EagleView: what it does and where it falls short

The long-standing default for measurement reports, and the one insurance adjusters are used to seeing.

Category
Service — Aerial roof measurement reports
Starting price
Priced per report — residential reports commonly from approx. $30, varying by report type
Best for
Contractors doing insurance restoration work who need carrier-accepted measurements
Not a good fit for
Contractors needing instant measurements at no marginal cost

What it does

EagleView produces measurement reports from aerial imagery. You submit a property address, and the report returns roof areas by facet, pitches, ridge, hip, valley, rake and eave lengths, a waste table and diagrams. That is enough to build a material order and a quote without a tape measure or a ladder, and without a second site visit.

Its practical advantage in restoration work is acceptance. Insurance carriers and adjusters have seen these reports for years, which makes them a workable common reference in a claim discussion rather than a number the contractor asserts. Reports also feed directly into most roofing CRMs and estimating tools, so measurements arrive as line items rather than retyped figures.

Who it suits

This suits contractors for whom measurement credibility is worth paying per job.

  • Insurance restoration contractors who need documentation an adjuster will accept without argument.
  • Companies estimating steep, complex or multi-facet roofs where hand measurement is slow and risky.
  • Shops wanting to quote from the office and reduce truck rolls to measure.
  • Teams whose CRM ingests the report data directly.

Where it falls short

Every remote measurement service shares the same underlying limits, and the cost is per job.

  • You pay per report. At high estimating volume with a low close rate, the cost of measuring jobs you do not win adds up quickly.
  • Reports are only as current as the imagery behind them. Recent additions, a re-roof, or a structure not yet captured produce a confidently wrong report.
  • Heavy tree cover, unusual geometry and low-slope or hidden sections are where accuracy degrades, and you are still responsible for the material order.
  • Turnaround is not instant, which is awkward if your sales process depends on quoting during the first visit.
  • Interior details a roof job depends on — decking condition, existing layers, ventilation adequacy — are invisible to it. It measures a shape, not a roof's condition.

Before you buy

Track your cost per report against your close rate, not per job sold. That is the number that decides whether pay-per-report or a subscription model is right for you.

Set a rule for when a physical verification is required anyway — heavy tree cover, additions, anything unusual — and hold to it.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
