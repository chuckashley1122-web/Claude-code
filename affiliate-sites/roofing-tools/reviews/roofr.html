Roofr: what it does and where it falls short

Aimed at the estimating and proposal end, priced to be accessible to a small shop.

Category
Software — Measurement reports and roofing proposals
Starting price
Free plan available; paid plans from approx. $99/month, measurement reports priced per report
Best for
Small roofing companies that want measurements and good-looking proposals cheaply
Not a good fit for
Large operations needing deep production management

What it does

Roofr's core is roof measurement: you submit an address and receive a report with areas, pitches, ridge and valley lengths and a diagram you can price from, without climbing the roof. On top of that sits a proposal builder that turns the measurement and your pricing into a presentable, e-signable document with options a homeowner can choose between.

The surrounding product has grown into a light CRM — lead capture including an instant-estimate website widget, a pipeline, job files, invoicing and payments. The positioning is deliberately the opposite of the enterprise platforms: published pricing, a usable free tier, and pay-per-report measurements so a small company's cost tracks its actual volume.

Who it suits

This is the accessible end of the roofing software market.

  • Newer or small roofing companies where a four-figure monthly software bill is not realistic.
  • Contractors whose main constraint is estimating speed and proposal quality rather than production management.
  • Companies with variable volume that prefer per-report measurement costs to a large fixed subscription.
  • Owners who want to try something without a sales call and an annual contract.

Where it falls short

It is strong at the front of the job and thin at the back.

  • Production management is light. Crew scheduling, material ordering depth and job costing do not match the full roofing CRMs.
  • Measurement reports are produced to a turnaround time rather than instantly, and complex or heavily obstructed roofs are where any remote measurement service is least reliable — verify before ordering material.
  • Imagery age matters. A recently re-roofed or altered house can measure against an outdated aerial image, and the error is yours to catch.
  • Insurance restoration workflow is not a focus, so claim-heavy companies will need something else alongside it.
  • Per-report pricing that helps a low-volume shop becomes a less attractive model at high estimating volume — check the crossover point against a subscription measurement service.

Before you buy

Order a report for a roof you have already measured by hand and compare. That single test tells you more about fit than any feature list.

Work out your monthly estimate count and compare pay-per-report against subscription alternatives at that volume.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
