CompanyCam: what it does and where it falls short

Does one thing — job photos that are actually findable later — and does it well.

Category
Software — Job photo documentation
Starting price
From approx. $19 per user/month (published tiers, free tier available)
Best for
Any crew-based contractor that argues about what the site looked like
Not a good fit for
Companies expecting it to manage jobs or money

What it does

CompanyCam replaces the camera roll. Photos taken in the app are automatically tagged with the project, time and location, so every image from a job lands in one chronological place that anyone at the company can find. Crews annotate directly on photos, record voice notes, complete photo checklists for each stage, and generate before-and-after galleries or PDF reports to send a homeowner or an adjuster.

For roofing the value is concentrated in three places: documenting pre-existing damage before you touch a roof, proving production stages for warranty and payment, and supporting insurance claims with time-stamped evidence. It integrates with the major roofing CRMs so photos attach to the job file in whatever system you already run.

Who it suits

It suits any company where photos currently live on individual phones.

  • Roofing crews needing documented proof of site condition before and after work.
  • Companies handling insurance claims that require time-stamped evidence.
  • Shops with multiple crews where the office needs to see progress without driving to site.
  • Teams already on a CRM that CompanyCam integrates with.

Where it falls short

It is a single-purpose tool, and priced per person.

  • It does not schedule, estimate, invoice or manage production. It is an addition to your stack, not a replacement for any of it.
  • Per-user pricing across a large crew count becomes a noticeable line item, and roofing headcount is seasonal.
  • Adoption is the whole battle. Crews that keep using the native camera produce a half-empty record, which is worse than a consistent one.
  • Heavy photo and video use consumes mobile data, and rural or shielded sites mean uploads queue rather than complete.
  • Being a separate system means another login and another place job information lives, unless the CRM integration is set up properly.

Before you buy

Decide your required photo checklist per job stage before rollout, and make it a condition of getting paid for the stage. Without that, adoption drifts.

Check the integration with your CRM specifically, including whether photos sync both ways, before assuming it is seamless.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
