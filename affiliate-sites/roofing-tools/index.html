Roofing contractor software and materials, compared

Roofing runs on two things: getting the job sold and getting the job produced. The software on this list attacks one or both, usually by cutting the time between a lead arriving and a signed contract, or by stopping jobs from falling through the cracks between sales and production.

The last three entries are materials rather than software, because the shingle or membrane you standardise on is as much an operational decision as your CRM — it drives your labour rates, your warranty position and what you can promise a homeowner. Each page covers what the product does, who it suits, and where it falls short.

How this list is put together

Entries are described from vendor documentation, published pricing, product data sheets and installation instructions. Where a vendor quotes per company instead of publishing rates, the table says so.

The list covers the decisions a roofing contractor actually faces rather than every product in the category:

  • Two full production CRMs, for companies that need one system from lead to final invoice.
  • Two aerial and photo measurement services, which is where most roofing estimating time goes.
  • Two point tools — photo documentation and proposals — that solve one problem well and sit alongside a CRM.
  • Three materials: two architectural asphalt shingle lines and one commercial single-ply membrane.
  • No product was included or excluded because of an affiliate relationship.
