Workiz: what it does and where it falls short

Built around the phone — which for a lot of plumbing companies is where the business actually happens.

Category
Software — Field service management with built-in phone system
Starting price
From approx. $200/month for a small team (published tiers vary by seat count)
Best for
Plumbing shops that live on inbound calls and want to know which advertising produced them
Not a good fit for
Companies whose work comes from contracts rather than inbound calls

What it does

Workiz covers standard field service ground — scheduling, a dispatch board, estimates, invoices, payments, a technician app and reporting — but its distinguishing feature is a built-in phone system. Calls come into the software, get recorded and attributed to a lead source, and pop the customer's record before anyone answers.

For a call-driven plumbing business, that produces two things worth money. First, marketing attribution that is real: you can see which number and which advert generated which booked job, and stop paying for the channels that do not. Second, call recordings you can use to coach whoever is answering — booking rate on inbound calls is often a bigger lever than anything happening in the field.

Who it suits

Workiz fits a company whose growth problem is the phone.

  • Residential plumbing shops spending real money on advertising and unsure which of it works.
  • Owners who want to review how calls are handled and improve booking rates.
  • Companies running several tracking numbers across different channels.
  • Shops that would otherwise be paying separately for a phone system and field software.

Where it falls short

The phone focus is the whole value proposition, which limits who it suits.

  • If your work comes from contracts, builders or repeat commercial clients rather than inbound calls, you are paying for the main feature and not using it.
  • Entry pricing is higher than the light platforms, and it climbs with seats — the total for a growing team gets close to heavier platforms with deeper functionality.
  • Field service depth outside the phone module is competent rather than leading; inventory, memberships and job costing are lighter than specialists.
  • Replacing your phone system is a real migration with real risk — number porting and call quality are not things you want to get wrong mid-season.
  • Call recording carries legal obligations that vary by state. Consent requirements are yours to get right, not the vendor's.

Before you buy

Check your own numbers first: what share of booked jobs started as an inbound call? If it is low, the main reason to buy this does not apply to you.

Confirm the call recording consent rules that apply where you operate before switching it on.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
