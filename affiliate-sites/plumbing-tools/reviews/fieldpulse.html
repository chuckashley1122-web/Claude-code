FieldPulse: what it does and where it falls short

Aims at the gap between starter apps and enterprise platforms, which is where a lot of plumbing companies actually sit.

Category
Software — Field service management, mid-market
Starting price
Quote-based; commonly positioned in the low hundreds per month for a small team
Best for
Growing plumbing companies that have outgrown entry software but cannot justify enterprise pricing
Not a good fit for
Companies wanting the deepest reporting in the category

What it does

FieldPulse covers the same ground as the better-known platforms — scheduling and dispatch, estimates and invoices, a price book, customer records with history, a technician mobile app, time tracking, payments and accounting sync — and adds mid-market pieces that entry-level tools skip, including job costing, subcontractor management and more structured customer communication.

Its positioning matters more than any single feature. Many plumbing companies are past the point where a starter app copes, but not at a size where an enterprise platform's cost and implementation effort make sense. FieldPulse targets that middle, offering more capability than the cheap tier without the multi-week onboarding project.

Who it suits

This is a step-up purchase rather than a first system.

  • Plumbing companies with roughly four to fifteen field staff feeling the limits of entry-level software.
  • Shops that need real job costing but do not need marketing attribution and call-centre tooling.
  • Companies using subcontractors who need them managed inside the system.
  • Owners who want more capability without a long implementation.

Where it falls short

It is less established than the platforms either side of it, and that has consequences.

  • Pricing is quote-based, so comparison requires a conversation even though it is not an enterprise product.
  • Smaller ecosystem than the market leaders — fewer third-party integrations, and less chance that a niche tool you use already connects.
  • Reporting is solid but not best-in-class; companies wanting deep marketing attribution or granular technician analytics will find the ceiling.
  • Being mid-market means some features feel like they are still filling out, and a capability gap can persist longer than with a larger vendor.
  • Migrating in from an entry platform still involves real data and price book work — lighter than enterprise onboarding, but not nothing.

Before you buy

List the three specific things your current software cannot do, and confirm each one in a demo using your own data. Vague dissatisfaction is a bad reason to migrate.

Ask about the integrations you actually depend on by name, not in general.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
