ServiceTitan: what it does and where it falls short

The heaviest platform on this list — built for a plumbing company that has become a business with departments.

Category
Software — All-in-one field service management
Starting price
Quote only — no public pricing
Best for
Residential plumbing companies with roughly ten or more technicians and an office team
Not a good fit for
Owner-operators and two-van shops

What it does

ServiceTitan runs the whole office loop. Calls are booked on a screen that shows the property's history and previous work, dispatch assigns and moves jobs on a live board, and the technician arrives with a tablet that presents flat-rate options, captures photos, records what was found, takes payment and submits a financing application at the kitchen table.

For plumbing specifically, the useful depth is in recurring revenue and reporting: membership plans with automatic renewal billing, maintenance visit scheduling, purchase orders and truck inventory, and reporting that shows average ticket and revenue per technician, plus which marketing spend produced which call. Job costing accounts for labour and material so you can see which work is actually profitable rather than which work is busiest.

Who it suits

This is a platform for a company managing people and margins, not just a calendar.

  • Residential service and replacement companies with enough daily call volume that dispatch efficiency is measurable money.
  • Owners who want per-technician performance numbers and will hold people to them.
  • Shops selling memberships and water heater or repipe replacements where option presentation and financing affect close rates.
  • Companies with someone whose job includes owning the software — maintaining the pricebook, building reports, training new technicians.

Where it falls short

Most disappointment with it comes from buying it before the company is ready to run it.

  • Quote-only pricing at the top of the market, usually with an implementation fee on top. You cannot compare it against alternatives without entering a sales process.
  • Typically annual contracts that do not scale down if you shrink. A slow quarter or losing two technicians does not reduce the bill.
  • Implementation is a multi-week project. Flat-rate pricebook build, data migration and technician training all have to happen before it earns anything.
  • Too heavy for a small shop. Whole areas of it assume a dedicated dispatcher and CSR; if that is you as well as the person under the sink, the extra steps hurt.
  • Its flexibility means many setup decisions are yours, and a badly configured pricebook or workflow is painful to unpick later.

Before you buy

Get four numbers in writing: total first-year cost including implementation, contract term, renewal pricing behaviour, and what each seat type costs. Those decide viability more than any demo.

Ask yourself who maintains the pricebook. If there is no honest answer, a lighter platform will serve you better.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
