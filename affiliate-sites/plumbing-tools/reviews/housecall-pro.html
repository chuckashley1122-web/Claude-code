Housecall Pro: what it does and where it falls short

The usual first real system for a small plumbing shop, and quick enough to be running this week.

Category
Software — Field service management for small teams
Starting price
From approx. $79/month (published tiers)
Best for
One to roughly ten technicians moving off paper and spreadsheets
Not a good fit for
Commercial plumbing with progress billing and complex POs

What it does

Housecall Pro covers the everyday cycle: take the call, put it on a calendar, send the customer an automatic confirmation and an on-the-way notification, have the technician build an estimate or invoice on a phone at the property, and collect a card payment before leaving. Automated follow-ups and review requests run themselves, and recurring service plans handle maintenance agreements.

Supporting that is a customer database with job and equipment history, reusable price book items, technician GPS and time tracking, QuickBooks sync, and basic reporting on revenue and job types. The whole product is built on the assumption that the person entering information is standing in a crawlspace, which is why small shops adopt it faster than heavier systems.

Who it suits

This is the right size of tool for a shop whose current system is a notebook.

  • Owner-operators and small crews who need scheduling, invoicing and card payments working immediately.
  • Residential service work with short, repeatable jobs — drain clears, fixture swaps, water heaters.
  • Owners who want published pricing they can evaluate without a sales call.
  • Companies where the owner is still on the tools and will use the app personally.

Where it falls short

It is built for speed of adoption, and that costs depth.

  • Job costing and reporting are shallow. Revenue and job counts are easy; true profit per job with burdened labour and overhead means exporting to a spreadsheet.
  • Features are spread across tiers and paid add-ons, so the advertised entry price is rarely what a working setup costs.
  • Commercial plumbing fits badly. Progress billing, retainage, lien waivers and demanding PO requirements are outside what it was designed for.
  • Inventory and purchasing are basic. Tracking stock across several vans and a warehouse usually needs something else.
  • Card processing fees are part of the true cost — compare the effective rate against your current merchant account rather than treating the convenience as free.

Before you buy

Price the tier that genuinely contains your required features, at your real seat count, plus expected card volume. That total is the comparison number.

Trial it in a busy week. The questions that matter are whether your least tech-comfortable technician will use it, and whether dispatch holds together when three calls move at once.

Check the current price and terms directly with the vendor. Pricing, tiers and stock change constantly, and the vendor's own page is the only reliable source.
