# Lending glossary — CA-J Consulting

**Status:** DRAFT. Not yet approved. `approved: false` in the knowledge manifest.

Plain-English definitions for educational content. Written to be quotable in
customer-facing drafts — so every entry is general, carries no figures, and
implies nothing about any particular borrower.

Where a term is commonly misunderstood, the entry says so. Those are the ones
worth writing content about.

---

## Cost of money

**Interest rate** — the cost of borrowing the principal, expressed as a
percentage per year. It does not include most fees, which is why it is usually
lower than the APR and why comparing rates alone is misleading.

**APR (annual percentage rate)** — the cost of credit as a yearly rate,
including the interest rate plus certain required fees. It exists so two offers
can be compared on one number. For consumer credit, how APR is calculated and
disclosed is prescribed by regulation.

**Factor rate** — used in some short-term business financing and merchant cash
advances. Expressed as a multiplier rather than a percentage: an advance with a
factor rate of 1.3 means the business repays 1.3 times the amount advanced.

> The 1.3 above is a **definitional illustration** — it explains what the
> mechanism is, not what anything costs. It is not a quote, not a range, and not
> representative of market pricing. Whether even this much is permitted in
> customer-facing content is flagged for the reviewer in
> `compliance-guardrails.md` §10 item 8.

> **Commonly misunderstood, and worth its own article.** A factor rate is not an
> interest rate and is not an APR. Because the full amount is owed regardless of
> how quickly it is repaid, the effective annualized cost of a factor-rate
> product is often dramatically higher than the multiplier suggests — and paying
> it off early does not reduce the total unless the contract specifically says
> so. Anyone comparing a factor-rate offer to an interest-rate offer is
> comparing two different things.

**Origination fee** — a fee for processing and underwriting the loan, often a
percentage of the amount borrowed. May be paid up front or deducted from
proceeds, which changes how much money actually arrives.

**Points** — fees paid at closing, each point generally equal to one percent of
the loan amount. In mortgage lending, discount points are paid to reduce the
interest rate; origination points are a fee for making the loan.

**Prepayment penalty** — a charge for paying off a loan early, or ahead of an
agreed schedule. Whether one exists, and how it is calculated, varies by product
and lender.

**Closing costs** — the total of fees due to complete a transaction: origination,
appraisal, title, recording, escrow setup, and others depending on the deal.

## Amount and structure

**Principal** — the amount borrowed, separate from interest and fees.

**Term** — how long the borrower has to repay.

**Amortization** — the schedule by which a loan is paid down over time. On a
fully amortizing loan, the scheduled payments retire the balance by the end of
the term. Early payments are typically mostly interest; later payments are
mostly principal.

**Balloon payment** — a large payment due at the end of a term, because the
scheduled payments did not fully amortize the balance. Common where the
amortization period is longer than the term.

**Draw period / revolving credit** — an arrangement where the borrower can draw,
repay, and draw again up to a limit, rather than receiving a single lump sum.

**Interest-only period** — a stretch during which payments cover interest only
and the principal balance does not decline.

## Underwriting measures

**Credit score** — a number summarizing credit history. Several scoring models
exist and lenders use different ones, so a score seen in a consumer app may not
match the score a lender pulls.

**DTI (debt-to-income ratio)** — monthly debt obligations as a share of monthly
gross income. Used mainly in consumer and mortgage lending.

**DSCR (debt service coverage ratio)** — cash available for debt service divided
by the debt service owed. Used in commercial and investment-property lending. A
ratio above 1.0 indicates income exceeds the obligation; lenders generally look
for a cushion above that.

**LTV (loan-to-value)** — the loan amount as a percentage of the value of the
collateral. **CLTV (combined loan-to-value)** includes all liens against the
same collateral.

**Time in business** — how long the business has operated. A common threshold
criterion in commercial lending, and frequently the reason a newer business is
directed toward different products.

**Working capital** — current assets minus current liabilities: the short-term
liquidity a business runs on.

## Security and guarantees

**Collateral** — an asset pledged to secure a loan, which the lender may claim
if the loan is not repaid.

**Secured vs unsecured** — secured lending is backed by specific collateral;
unsecured is not. Unsecured credit generally carries higher cost, because the
lender has less recourse.

**Lien** — a legal claim against an asset securing a debt.

**UCC-1 financing statement** — a filing that publicly records a lender's
security interest in a business's assets. A **blanket lien** covers
substantially all business assets rather than a specific item.

**Personal guarantee** — a promise by an individual to repay a business debt
personally if the business does not.

> **Commonly misunderstood.** A personal guarantee sits outside the corporate
> structure. Forming an LLC or corporation does not protect a guarantor from a
> debt they personally guaranteed. This is one of the most consequential terms
> in small-business lending and one of the least read.

**Subordination** — an agreement setting which lender is repaid first when more
than one has a claim on the same collateral.

**Cross-collateralization** — where one pledged asset secures more than one
obligation.

## Process

**Prequalification** — an informal, usually unverified indication based on
information the borrower supplies. It is not an approval and not a commitment.

**Preapproval** — a more substantive review, still conditional. What the term
means varies by lender and by product, which is exactly why it should never be
described as a decision.

**Underwriting** — the lender's evaluation of the risk: the borrower, the
business, the collateral, and the structure.

**Term sheet / letter of intent** — a summary of proposed terms. Generally not
a binding commitment to lend; read what it actually says.

**Commitment letter** — a lender's agreement to lend on stated terms, usually
subject to conditions.

**Conditions / stipulations** — items that must be satisfied before funding.

**Covenant** — an ongoing obligation in a loan agreement. **Affirmative**
covenants require doing something, such as providing financial statements.
**Negative** covenants prohibit something, such as taking on additional debt.

**Default** — failure to meet the loan's terms. Note that default is not only
missed payments — breaching a covenant can constitute default even while
payments are current.

**Escrow** — funds held by a third party. In mortgage lending, commonly an
account collecting property taxes and insurance alongside the payment.

**PITI** — principal, interest, taxes, and insurance: the components of a
typical escrowed mortgage payment.

## Business lending specifics

**SBA guaranty** — under Small Business Administration programs, the SBA
guarantees a portion of a loan made by a participating lender. The SBA is
generally not the lender.

> **Commonly misunderstood.** An SBA loan is applied for through a bank or other
> participating lender, not from the SBA directly. Program rules, eligibility,
> and fee structures are set by the SBA and change — always verify current
> program details rather than relying on any summary, including this one.

**Guaranty fee** — a fee associated with SBA-guaranteed lending. Amounts and
applicability are set by program rules and change over time.

**Merchant cash advance (MCA)** — a purchase of a portion of future receivables
rather than a loan. Because it is structured as a purchase, it may fall outside
some lending regulations. Typically repaid through a fixed daily or weekly
remittance or a percentage of card receipts. Generally among the most expensive
forms of business financing.

**Invoice factoring** — selling outstanding invoices to a third party at a
discount for immediate cash. **Recourse** factoring leaves the business liable
if the customer does not pay; **non-recourse** shifts some of that risk, subject
to the contract's definitions.

**Accounts receivable financing** — borrowing against outstanding invoices while
retaining ownership of them. Distinct from factoring, though the terms are often
used loosely.

**Holdback** — the portion of daily or weekly receipts remitted to the funder
under a receivables-based product.

**Equipment financing** — lending secured by the equipment being purchased,
where the equipment itself typically serves as the collateral.

## Mortgage specifics

**Fixed-rate** — the interest rate does not change over the life of the loan.

**Adjustable-rate (ARM)** — the rate adjusts periodically according to an index
plus a margin, subject to caps. Usually described by an initial fixed period
followed by an adjustment frequency.

**Index and margin** — an ARM's rate is a published index plus a fixed margin
set by the lender.

**Caps** — limits on how much an ARM rate may move at one adjustment and over
the life of the loan.

**Conforming loan** — a mortgage meeting the criteria to be purchased by the
government-sponsored enterprises, including a maximum loan amount that is set
annually and varies by county. **Jumbo** exceeds that limit.

**Escrow account** — see Escrow above.

**Mortgage insurance** — insurance protecting the lender where the borrower's
equity is below a threshold. Requirements, cost, and how it ends differ by
program.

**Rate-and-term refinance** — replacing an existing mortgage with a new one on
different terms. **Cash-out refinance** — replacing it with a larger loan and
taking the difference in cash.

**HELOC (home equity line of credit)** — revolving credit secured by the equity
in a property, typically with a draw period followed by a repayment period.

**Appraisal** — an independent opinion of a property's value.

**Title insurance** — insurance against defects in the ownership record.
