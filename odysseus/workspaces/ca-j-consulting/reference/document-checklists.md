## Business lending — the common core

Most business loan types ask for some version of this set. What changes between
products is what gets added on top.

| Document | Why lenders generally ask |
|---|---|
| Business tax returns, recent years | Independent confirmation of revenue and profitability |
| Personal tax returns for significant owners | Where a personal guarantee is contemplated |
| Profit & loss statement | Current-period performance between tax filings |
| Balance sheet | Assets, liabilities, and net worth of the business |
| Business bank statements | Actual cash flow, as opposed to reported |
| Debt schedule | Existing obligations, so total debt service can be assessed |
| Personal financial statement | Where a guarantee is contemplated |
| Business formation documents | Confirms the entity exists and who owns it |
| EIN confirmation | Ties the entity to its tax filings |
| Business licence or registration | Confirms the business may legally operate |
| Ownership breakdown | Identifies who must sign or guarantee |
| Government photo ID for signers | Identity verification |

## By loan type — what gets added

### SBA 7(a) and 504

The core set, plus SBA program forms and eligibility material. SBA-guaranteed
lending is documentation-heavy because eligibility is set by program rules
rather than by the lender.

Typically added: SBA application and personal history forms, a personal
financial statement on the SBA's own form, a use-of-proceeds breakdown,
affiliate business information, and — for 504 or real-estate use — the purchase
contract and property documentation.

**Form numbers and requirements change.** Never cite a specific SBA form number
in published content without confirming it is current.

### Conventional term loan

The core set, plus a stated purpose and, where the loan funds a specific
project, supporting quotes or contracts. Some lenders ask for projections.

### Business line of credit

The core set, plus accounts receivable and accounts payable aging reports, and
inventory reports where inventory secures the line. Lines are usually reviewed
periodically, so expect updated financials on a recurring basis.

### Equipment financing

Lighter than most, because the equipment secures the loan. Typically the core
set in abbreviated form, plus the vendor quote or invoice, equipment
specifications, and delivery or installation details.

### Invoice factoring and AR financing

Underwriting leans on the customers rather than the business, so the emphasis
shifts: accounts receivable aging, a customer list with payment history,
sample invoices and the contracts behind them, and formation documents.
Personal and business tax returns matter less here than in most products.

### Commercial real estate

The core set, plus property-specific documentation: the purchase contract,
rent roll and current leases for income property, operating statements for the
property, an appraisal, environmental reports, title work, and insurance.

### Merchant cash advance

Usually the lightest documentation of any product — commonly recent bank
statements and card processing statements, plus formation documents and ID.

**Light documentation is not the same as low cost.** Content covering this
product should make that plain: the speed and simplicity are what is being paid
for. See `loan-types.md` for the cost structure.

## Mortgage lending — typical categories

Described as categories, not a checklist, because residential mortgage
requirements are program-specific and change.

**Income.** Recent pay stubs; W-2s or 1099s; tax returns, particularly where
income is self-employed, commission, or variable; and documentation of any
other income being counted.

**Assets.** Recent bank and investment statements; documentation of the source
of funds for a down payment; and a gift letter with a paper trail where any
part of the funds is a gift.

**Identity and credit.** Government photo ID; Social Security number
authorisation for the credit pull; and written explanation of significant
credit events.

**Property.** Purchase contract; appraisal; homeowner's insurance; and for a
condominium, documentation about the association.

**Self-employed borrowers** are generally asked for more: additional years of
returns, year-to-date business financials, and confirmation the business is
active.

> **This workspace never receives any of these.** Mortgage documentation is
> concentrated PII — identifiers, income, and assets in one place. See

## Two things worth explaining, not just listing

A checklist tells someone what to gather. Content that also explains *why*
tends to be more useful and more read.

**Why lenders want both business and personal returns.** In small-business
lending the owner's finances and the business's are frequently entangled, and a
personal guarantee makes the owner's position directly relevant.

**Why bank statements matter more than financial statements.** Statements are
produced by a third party. Financial statements are produced by the borrower.
Lenders read them together for that reason.
